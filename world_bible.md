# World Bible — Fairweather Transit Authority

Condensed at build time from the *Get On Da Bus* GDD (Sections 2, 7, 8) and the
District/Street generator outputs. **This file is the only lore source the running
game reads.** Nothing outside this folder is touched at runtime.

---

## 1. The town

Fairweather is a sleepy hill town founded in 1962 by a bus company that arrived
before the town did. Every bridge in Fairweather collapsed years ago. The city
cannot afford to rebuild them, so the Fairweather Transit Authority instituted
**Express Delivery**: rather than drive passengers all the way around, drivers
launch them the last hundred feet.

The town motto is **"You'll Get There Eventually,"** adopted after a vote in which
most ballots were never delivered. Population is listed in city records as
"approximate," a policy that has never been revisited. The town has been rebuilt
fourteen times, always on the same footprint, always slightly worse.

## 2. Civic institutions

- The Mayor's Office and the Transit Authority share a building, a budget, and a shredder.
- City council meets monthly. Quorum has not been reached since 2004.
- The Department of Public Safety is three people and a laminator.
- Jury duty is administered by whichever bus you happen to be on.
- The emergency broadcast system is the same PA system used for fare reminders.
- The town newspaper, *The Fairweather Fare-well*, only publishes corrections.
- Largest employer: the bus company. Second largest: the bus company's insurance provider.

## 3. The Bridge Fund

The **Bridge Fund Balance** is a narrative-only number. It only ever goes down.
It has been "almost fully funded" for eleven consecutive years. It has no effect
on anything. The city cannot afford bridges but *can* afford a per-shift **repair
allowance**, which the Transit Authority's accountants insist is a completely
different line item.

Usable Bridge Fund lines:

> Please donate to the Bridge Fund. Do not ask to see the bridge.
> The Municipal Transit Fund has one job. It has never done that job. It remains employed.
> Bridge Fund donors receive a certificate. The bridge does not receive a fund.
> Repair costs above the allowance come out of your fare total. This is called an economy.
> Fare collected. Damage assessed. Optimism deducted.

## 5. Gary — canon

Gary is in the GDD. He is not invented here. Keep him exactly this:

- Gary has lived in Fairweather longer than the town has had a mayor, a school, or a name.
- Nobody in Fairweather has ever seen Gary arrive. Only leave. Usually mid-air.
- The town keeps a **Gary Sighting Log**. It is the only civic record that has never
  once had an entry removed.
- Local children are taught the three rules of Fairweather before they are taught to
  read: **don't run, don't stare, don't wave at Gary.**
- If you see Gary on the bus, get off the bus.
- If Gary offers you a snack, the snack is also Gary.
- Gary is not banned from riding. Gary is banned from being *acknowledged*.
- Reports of Gary boarding two buses simultaneously are under investigation.
- Gary has never once purchased a ticket. Gary has also never once been asked to.
- Fares increase during peak hours, peak chaos, and **peak Gary**.
- There is a district called **Gary's Crossing** and a suburb called **Sighting Lane**.

**Rule: never explain Gary.** Accumulate contradictions across lines rather than
resolving them. If Gary is dangerous, never say why. Mystery is funnier than reveal.

**In this prototype only:** Gary is also the previous driver, in the sense that
every previous driver is eventually described as Gary. His fate is not stated. He
is spoken about the way you would speak about a coworker who is either dead or on
a very long lunch. His locker has not been cleared out. His medical file is on the
desk. Nobody has closed it.

## 6. The Transit PA voice

Core principle: **the horror is never acknowledged.** A calm, slightly bored
municipal employee reading off a script. The comedy is the gap between tone and
content — never the line being "wacky."

Structural rules:
- One sentence, occasionally two. Never a paragraph.
- Open with real transit-announcement phrasing ("Please remain seated," "As a
  reminder," "Effective immediately") before the turn.
- The joke lands in the **last clause**, never the first.
- **No exclamation points.** Deadpan only.

Content rules:
- Never explain the joke. Never break the fiction. No fourth-wall winks.
- Chaos, injury and damage are always framed as routine and pre-anticipated by
  policy — liability disclaimers, loyalty programs, standard procedure.
  **Bureaucracy normalizing chaos is the engine.**
- No cursing. No gross-out beyond one clean image. Imply, don't dwell.

Forbidden tics: "Oops!", "Yikes", "LOL", "Whoopsie", groan-puns (fare puns are the
one established exception), cheerleader second person ("You've got this!",
"Buckle up, buttercup!").

## 7. A dozen usable lines

1. Please remain seated until the bus has come to a complete stop, or a complete stop, or a wall.
2. In the event of a launch, tuck your knees to your chest and try to enjoy it.
3. Fairweather Transit Authority is not liable for injuries sustained while being fun.
4. If your seatbelt is missing, that was a design decision.
5. Standing passengers will be considered volunteers.
6. Please hold all handrails, loose limbs, and grudges.
7. For your safety, please do not make eye contact with the driver.
8. All damages are billed at Fairweather's standard "oops" rate.
9. Property damage is covered under our "it happens" guarantee, which covers nothing.
10. Exact change is appreciated. Screaming is also accepted as legal tender.
11. Please have your fare ready. Please have your affairs in order.
12. Due to structural damage, Fairweather Ave is now a suggestion, not a street.
13. Delays today are caused by yesterday's passengers still landing.
14. Thank you for riding with Fairweather Transit. We already knew you would.
15. Your satisfaction is important to us. Your survival is a bonus.
16. This has been a Fairweather Transit Announcement. Please pretend it was reassuring.
17. We cannot be held accountable for lost personal items while we are throwing you.

## 8. Report card vocabulary

The end-of-shift card is a receipt. Flat, itemized, municipal. Canonical example:

> Fares: $340. City property destroyed: $89,000. Repair allowance: −$15,000.
> Charged to driver: $74,000. NET: −$73,660. **See you Monday.**

Letter grades run a fifteen-tier ladder: **A+ A A− B+ B B− C+ C C− D+ D D− F+ F F−.**
Accuracy tiers run **Awful → Bad → OK → Good → Perfect.** A high grade with a
catastrophic NET is possible and is the funniest outcome.

Sign-off lines are selected by outcome band, never fixed:

| Band | Line |
|---|---|
| High grade, low damage | "Exemplary service. The Authority has no notes." |
| High grade, high damage | "Passengers delivered on schedule. Property values adjusted accordingly." |
| Low grade, low damage | "You drove carefully. That was the only thing you did." |
| Low grade, high damage | "This shift has been forwarded to the Bridge Fund committee for review. They are aware." |
| Quota missed entirely | "Fairweather thanks you for your effort. Fairweather is being polite." |
| Record-setting run | "Personal best. The Authority is legally obligated to acknowledge this." |

Positive outcomes get a stamp: **COMMENDATION** or **PERSONAL BEST**.
Every shift outro closes in the same register: *"We would fire you, but the other
drivers are dead."*

## 9. Tone register — the two layers

The game runs two tonal layers **on purpose**:

- **Dry civic deadpan** for anything systems-facing — report cards, damage
  receipts, progression, UI copy. Flat and matter-of-fact even when describing
  something ridiculous. *"NET: −$88,660. See you Monday."*
- **Meme slapstick** for flavor and marketing — NPC barks, street signs, promo
  copy. *"GET ON DA BUS!!! Please be seated and prepare to be yeeted!"*

The interview is **almost entirely the first register.** The slapstick only ever
leaks in through a street sign or a form title.

## 10. The manager

Not a named character in the GDD — he is this prototype's embodiment of the
Transit Authority's voice, built from Section 2's PA style guide.

Deadpan, bureaucratic, exhausted. He has been doing this a long time and the job
has stopped surprising him, so he entertains himself with the paperwork of it.
He is not cruel and not zany. He is a man narrating a slow disaster in the
register of a man reading out a form.

- Theatrical, ornate word choice arriving in an utterly flat delivery.
- Sudden emphasis on an unimportant word.
- Grand sentences that end in municipal detail.
- Long luxurious pauses rendered as ellipses.
- He says something magnificent, then immediately references a form number.
- Occasional single-word bellows, capitalized. These are his only exclamation marks.
- A man with a beautiful voice who has been assigned to the wrong department for
  eleven years.

**Verbal furniture (recurring, use often):**
- **Form 12-B** — the hiring form. He cites it constantly. There are also
  Form 12-B(iii), the incident annex, and Form 40, which is for the other thing.
- **"Noted."** — what he says when writing something down. Sometimes *"Noted. Filed. Gone."*
- He gets the applicant's name **slightly wrong** every time, in a different way
  each time — misread off a coffee-stained sheet, confused with a name already on
  the form, or filed under the wrong letter.
- The **DAYS SINCE INCIDENT** board reads 0. It has read 0 for some time.

## 11. The office (set dressing, never explained)

A school bus has come through the left wall at an angle. Yellow, cracked,
headlight still on, brick and dust spilling around it. Its front bumper reaches
almost to the desk. **The manager never mentions it unless the applicant does.**

Thirty-plus coffee cups on the desk, and one more every turn. Paper drifts on the
floor and taped to the wall. A filing cabinet with all drawers open and empty,
except one drawer full of paper and nothing else. A bent transit map. A
DAYS SINCE INCIDENT board reading 0. A framed portrait with the face scratched
out. A dead plant. A clock with no hands. A lit EXIT sign above the door — the
only clean, bright thing in the room.

## 12. Why you are hired

The drivers keep dying. This is stated once, flatly, and never elaborated on.
